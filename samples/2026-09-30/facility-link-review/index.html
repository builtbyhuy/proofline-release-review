What the checks can and cannot tell you

Checks flag empty demo labels, malformed or disallowed URLs, and repeated normalized destinations. Duplicate URLs may be intentional; an owner must decide. Passing these checks only means the local records meet these rules.

There is no verification of HTTP status, redirects, DNS, TLS, page content, WCAG conformance, visible labels, ARIA names or production behavior. There are no known correct replacement destinations to apply.

Practice changes: F01 gets an empty demo label; F02 points to F01’s destination; F03 gets a deliberately invalid javascript: URL. The original source manifest never changes. Return to Published references to reset.

Could this save review time?

Illustrative workload assumption: six links × two minutes of manual review = 12 minutes per review; three reviews a month = 36 minutes. Those are not measured Facility figures or a net-savings claim.

This fixture cannot replace opening destinations or checking accessibility. Any actual saving would need a timed comparison that includes setup, false-positive review and the live checks still required. No revenue or cash saving is claimed.
